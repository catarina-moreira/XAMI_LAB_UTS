This is the lab's foundational theme: building the algorithms, theory and evaluation
protocols that make machine intelligence explainable, transparent, fair and
understandable to human decision-makers.

Modern predictive models are applied as "black boxes" whose internal representations are
too complex for a human to understand. We develop methods that open these boxes — and,
just as importantly, ways to *measure* whether an explanation can actually be trusted.

![Counterfactual explanations](/assets/project/Counterfactual.png)

## Counterfactual and causal explanations

"What-if" explanations — *what would I have to change for my loan application to be
accepted?* — are the bridge between machine-learned statistics and human reasoning. We
build on the hypothesis that causality is the crucial missing ingredient for
human-understandable explanations: correlation-based explainers are sensitive to sampling
bias and can be more biased than the black box itself. Work in this line includes
counterfactual algorithms, causal inference from observational data, interpretable
probabilistic models such as Bayesian networks, and Bayesian causal discovery for
decision making.

## Evaluating and contesting explanations

An explanation is only useful if it is faithful, stable and actionable. We develop
evaluation protocols and benchmarks for explainable methods — fidelity and stability
metrics, functionally-grounded evaluation guidelines, and large-scale benchmarking of
counterfactual algorithms — and study how AI systems can be made *contestable*, so the
people affected by automated decisions can question and challenge them. This extends to
human-centric explanation: persuasive, narrative explanations that measurably change
user understanding and trust, with human-grounded evaluation protocols.

## Robustness and fairness

Trustworthy AI must also withstand manipulation. We investigate adversarial attacks on
tabular data and their imperceptibility, benchmark model robustness, and design methods
to detect bias and support ethical, fair analyses of machine-learned predictions.

## Flagship application: explainable predictive process analytics

A long-running application of this theme is predictive process analytics — predicting
future states of business processes from event logs. We pioneered interpretability in
this space: interpretable process predictive models, XAI-enabled model inspection,
milestone-aware counterfactuals for process predictions, and multi-level explanations
for process outcomes, applied in healthcare, agri-food, government, finance and
industrial IoT.
