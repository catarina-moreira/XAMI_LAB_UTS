Healthcare is the lab's largest application domain — and the one where the cost of an
unexplained prediction is highest. This theme builds AI that supports clinicians rather
than replacing their judgement: models that fuse the same signals a physician uses,
explain their reasoning, and are evaluated with clinical practice in mind.

![Explainable medical AI](/assets/project/MedicalXAI.png)

## Multimodal medical imaging

Radiologists never read an image in isolation — they combine it with the patient's
clinical context. We design deep learning architectures that do the same: fusing chest
X-rays with clinical data (MDF-Net), integrating radiologists' eye-gaze into abnormality
detection (EyeXNet), and augmenting clinical records with large language models (DALL-M).
We also build and publish the datasets this research needs, including MIMIC-Eye and
MIMICEL.

## Understanding and supporting clinical work

Using eye tracking, we study how radiologists actually examine medical images — their
visual search patterns, directional dynamics and moments of silent analysis — and use
those insights to build human-machine teaming approaches for diagnosis and training.

## Clinical decision support across specialties

Beyond radiology, the theme spans diagnostic AI for vestibular disorders (automated
video-nystagmography analysis, separating stroke from vestibular neuritis),
neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer's and Parkinson's detection from language, gait and
drawing), cancer risk stratification from whole-slide imaging and multi-omics data,
medical question answering with language models, and privacy-preserving federated
learning for clinical data.

Any work in the lab that serves medicine lives in this theme — whatever the method,
from vision transformers to LLMs.
