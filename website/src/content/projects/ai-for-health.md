Healthcare is where the XAMI mission matters most: an unexplained prediction in medicine
carries the highest possible cost. This theme builds AI that supports clinicians rather
than replacing their judgement, with models that use the same signals a physician uses,
explain their reasoning, and are evaluated against real clinical practice.

## Multimodal medical imaging

Radiologists never read an image in isolation; they combine it with the patient's
clinical context. We design deep learning architectures that do the same: fusing chest
X-rays with clinical data (MDF-Net), incorporating radiologists' eye gaze into
abnormality detection (EyeXNet), and augmenting clinical records with large language
models (DALL-M). We also build and publish the datasets this research needs, including
MIMIC-Eye and MIMICEL.

## Learning from clinical expertise

Using eye tracking, we study how radiologists actually examine medical images: their
visual search patterns, directional dynamics and moments of silent analysis. These
insights feed back into explainable models and into human-machine teaming approaches for
diagnosis and radiology training, so that AI explanations match how clinicians really
reason.

## Clinical decision support across specialties

Beyond radiology, the theme spans diagnostic AI for vestibular disorders, early detection
of neurodegenerative disease from language, gait and drawing, cancer risk stratification
from whole-slide imaging and multi-omics data, medical question answering with language
models, and privacy-preserving federated learning for sensitive clinical data.

Any work in the lab that serves medicine lives in this theme, whatever the method, and
always with the same requirement: the clinician must be able to understand and contest
what the model says.
